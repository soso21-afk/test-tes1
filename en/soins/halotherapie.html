Frequently asked questions

Good to know

What is halotherapy?

Halotherapy, or salt therapy, is a natural method that involves breathing air saturated with salt micro-particles. It usually takes place in rooms known as salt caves or salt rooms, where the walls are covered with natural salt and a halogenerator disperses salt particles into the air. The therapy aims to improve respiratory and skin conditions and to provide deep relaxation.

What are the benefits of halotherapy?

Halotherapy offers many potential benefits, including: relief from respiratory problems such as asthma, bronchitis, sinusitis and respiratory allergies; support for skin conditions such as psoriasis and eczema; reduced stress and anxiety, thanks to the calm and soothing environment of the salt cave; and immune-system support, as exposure to salt may stimulate the immune system and improve overall health.

What happens during a halotherapy session?

A halotherapy session takes place in a salt cave or salt room. Participants sit or lie down in a comfortable environment while the halogenerator releases fine salt particles into the air. Sessions generally last between 30 and 60 minutes. You can breathe deeply and relax during the session, sometimes while listening to soft music or meditating.

Is halotherapy safe?

Halotherapy is generally considered safe for most people, including children and the elderly. However, it may not be recommended for people with certain health conditions, such as hyperthyroidism, some kidney diseases or severe lung disorders. We recommend consulting a health professional before starting therapy if you have specific concerns.

How many sessions are needed to see results?

The number of sessions depends on individual needs and the conditions being treated. Some people feel the benefits after a single session, while others benefit from regular sessions for optimal results, especially for chronic conditions.

Is halotherapy covered by insurance?

Halotherapy is usually considered a wellness therapy rather than a medical treatment, and it is generally not covered by basic insurance. However, some supplementary insurance policies may cover part of the cost, particularly when it is recommended by a health professional.
