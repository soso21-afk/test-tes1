Frequently asked questions

Good to know

What is cryotherapy?

Cryotherapy is a therapeutic treatment that exposes the body to extremely low temperatures for a short period. It is used to reduce inflammation, relieve pain and improve physical recovery. There are two main forms of cryotherapy: localised cryotherapy and whole-body cryotherapy.

What are the benefits of cryotherapy?

The main benefits of cryotherapy include: reduced inflammation in muscles and joints; pain relief, effective for both chronic and acute pain, including pain from arthritis or sports injuries; faster sports recovery after intense exercise or competition; and firmer skin, as it can help improve skin elasticity and reduce the signs of ageing.

What happens during a cryotherapy session?

During a whole-body cryotherapy session, you enter a specially designed chamber where you are exposed to extremely low temperatures, generally between −110°C and −140°C, for 2 to 3 minutes. For localised cryotherapy, a device is used to apply cold air to specific areas of the body. Patients wear protective items such as gloves, socks and underwear to protect sensitive parts of the body.

Is cryotherapy painful?

Cryotherapy is generally not painful. You may feel an intense cold sensation, but it is usually well tolerated and fades quickly once the session ends.

How many cryotherapy sessions are needed to see results?

The number of sessions depends on each person's goals. Some people feel the benefits after a single session, while others may need regular sessions for lasting results, particularly for chronic pain management or improved sports performance.

Is cryotherapy safe?

Cryotherapy is generally safe for most people when performed under the supervision of a qualified professional. However, it may not be suitable for people with certain medical conditions such as cardiovascular disorders, circulation problems or cold allergies. We recommend consulting a doctor before starting cryotherapy treatment.
