Frequently asked questions

Good to know

What is cupping?

Cupping is an ancient therapeutic technique that involves applying suction cups to the skin to create a vacuum. The method is used to improve blood circulation, reduce pain and promote tissue healing.

What types of cups are used in cupping?

There are several types of cups: fire cups, used traditionally, create suction by heating the air inside the cup before placing it on the skin; air cups use a manual pump to create suction without heat; and silicone cups are flexible, easy to adjust and often used for dynamic massage.

What health issues can cupping help with?

Cupping is used to address various health issues, including: muscle and joint pain, muscle tension, respiratory problems (asthma, bronchitis), inflammation, digestive issues, and fatigue and stress.

What happens during a cupping session?

During a cupping session, the practitioner places the cups on specific points of the body, often on the back. The cups stay in place for a few minutes, creating suction that gently lifts the skin and underlying tissues. A session can last from 5 to 20 minutes depending on the treatment and how you respond.

Is cupping painful?

Most people feel a pulling or pressure sensation during the session, but it is generally not painful. Some people may feel slight discomfort or warmth. Temporary circular marks may appear on the skin after the session, but they usually fade within a few days.

Are there any side effects or contraindications to cupping?

Possible side effects include temporary bruising and skin sensitivity. Cupping is generally safe when performed by a qualified professional, but it may not be suitable for people with clotting disorders, sensitive skin or open wounds. We always recommend consulting a practitioner before starting treatment.
