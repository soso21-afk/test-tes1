Frequently asked questions

Good to know

What is massage?

Massage is a therapeutic technique that involves manipulating the body's muscles, tendons and connective tissues to improve circulation, relieve tension and promote relaxation. There are many types of massage, each with its own techniques and goals.

What are the most common types of massage?

Swedish massage uses long, flowing strokes to promote relaxation and improve circulation. Deep-tissue massage targets the deeper layers of muscle and connective tissue to release chronic tension. Sports massage is designed for athletes, helping to prepare the muscles for activity and recover after exertion. Thai massage combines deep pressure and passive stretching to improve flexibility and energy.

What are the benefits of massage?

Massage offers a variety of benefits, including: stress reduction, lowering cortisol levels and inducing deep relaxation; pain relief, effective for muscle and joint pain; improved blood and lymphatic circulation, helping to eliminate toxins and support immune function; improved flexibility, by stretching and releasing tight muscles; and reduced muscle tension, easing spasms and muscular pain.

What happens during a massage session?

During a massage session, you usually lie on a massage table in a calm, comfortable room. The massage therapist uses oils or lotions to help their hands glide over the skin. The pressure and techniques used are adapted to your needs and preferences. A session generally lasts between 30 and 90 minutes.

Is massage safe?

Massage is generally safe for most people, but there may be contraindications for certain medical conditions such as recent injuries, skin conditions or heart problems. It is important to discuss your state of health with the massage therapist before the session.

How many massage sessions are needed to see results?

The number of sessions depends on your individual goals and needs. Some people feel immediate benefits after a single session, while others benefit from regular massages for cumulative effects.
