Discover why this strange practice will make you float (and not just a little)

Floatation, a unique and relaxing experience, is growing in popularity for its many benefits for body and mind. Whether in a sensory-isolation pool or out in nature, this practice offers a moment of well-being accessible to everyone. Let's discover together why floating deserves to be tried at least once in a lifetime.

Floatation offers a unique experience of relaxation and well-being, with many benefits for body and mind. Here are the key points to remember:

Floating produces exceptional sensations of lightness and weightlessness, fostering deep relaxation

It offers many physical and mental benefits, such as reduced stress and better sleep

The associated thermal-contrast therapy stimulates circulation and strengthens the immune system

Floating can be incorporated into daily life in many ways, in a specialised centre or through alternative practices

Whether you are drawn to the calm of a floatation pod or the open water, here is what makes this practice so special.

The unique sensations of floating

Floating produces exceptional sensations of lightness and freedom. Whether on a surfboard, a paddleboard or in a specialised pool, the experience of being carried by water is incomparable. This feeling of gliding and weightlessness lets you disconnect from daily life and reach a state of deep relaxation.

Water-sports enthusiasts often speak of the liberating effect of floating. On a small surfboard or wing foil, you fully feel the movements of the water, which considerably improves balance and coordination. This intimate connection with the water offers a unique sensory experience, stimulating all the senses and fostering a heightened awareness of your body.

Here are some sensations commonly reported during a floatation session:

The impression of floating in space

Deep muscle release

Heightened awareness of your breathing

A feeling of warmth and well-being

Mental and emotional letting-go

These experiences vary from person to person, but they all contribute to a general feeling of relaxation and renewal.

Physical and mental benefits of the practice

Floating offers many advantages for physical and mental health. One of the most interesting aspects is the alternation between hot and cold, which is particularly beneficial for the body. This thermal-contrast therapy stimulates blood circulation by triggering successive vasodilation and vasoconstriction of the vessels.

The practice helps reduce inflammation and relieve muscle pain, making it an excellent complement to intense sports activities. Furthermore, cold exposure strengthens the immune system by increasing the production of white blood cells, improving the body's natural defences.

On the mental side, floating has remarkable effects:

Reduced stress and anxiety

Improved sleep quality

Increased creativity and concentration

Greater self-confidence

Better body awareness

The hot-cold alternation also stimulates the release of endorphins, the well-being hormones, which helps improve mood and general vitality. This combination of effects makes floating a holistic practice, beneficial for both body and mind.

How to try floating in the best conditions

To get the most out of your floating experience, it is essential to prepare well and choose the right conditions. Whether you opt for a session in a sensory-isolation pool or an outdoor water activity, some principles remain the same.

Here is a summary of the things to consider for an optimal floating session:

Duration: start with 60-minute sessions. Frequency: once or twice a week for noticeable results. Time of day: prefer the morning or evening for more calm. Food: avoid heavy meals before the session. Hydration: drink enough before and after the session.

For those who want to try thermal-contrast therapy, we recommend starting with 10 to 15 minutes of hot bathing, followed by 1 to 2 minutes of cold exposure. This alternation can be repeated several times to maximise the benefits. However, note that this practice may not be suitable for people with cardiovascular problems. It is advisable to consult a health professional before starting.

If you opt for an experience in a natural setting, such as surfing or paddleboarding, choose a spot suited to your level and make sure you are accompanied by a qualified instructor for your first sessions. Safety remains paramount to fully enjoy the benefits of floating in open water.

Making floating part of your routine

Incorporating floating into your routine may seem intimidating at first, but its long-term benefits are worth it. Whether you choose to practise in a specialised centre or take up water sports, there are many ways to bring this experience into your daily life.

For those without access to a floatation centre or a natural body of water, some of the benefits can be recreated at home. For example, you can alternate between a hot bath and a cold shower to stimulate your blood circulation and enjoy the effects of contrast therapy. This practice, though different from pure floating, nevertheless offers similar advantages in terms of relaxation and well-being.

Here are some suggestions for gradually integrating floating into your life:

Start with a monthly session in an isolation pool

Try paddleboarding or surfing on your next holiday

Practise "floating" guided meditation at home

Join a local water-sports club

Create a weekly ritual of alternating hot and cold baths

By adopting floating as a regular practice, you will notice an overall improvement in your quality of life. This unique experience will help you develop a better connection with your body, reduce your daily stress and explore new sensations of well-being. Don't hesitate any longer — let yourself be carried by the benefits of floating and discover why it deserves to be tried at least once in your life.
